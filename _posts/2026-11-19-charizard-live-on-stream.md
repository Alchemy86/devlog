<section class="prose" markdown="1">

The short is a stream layout: Charizard on the webcam at the top in a gaming headset, his
game in the middle, chat along the bottom. Underneath it all is a real streamer's sound
track, and everything Charizard does is cut to it.

The headset is new parts on his head bone: a band, two cups with green rings and a mic boom
to the mouth. Because they hang off the head bone, they go wherever his head goes.

The game is real. It is a Pokémon Blue cartridge running in TerminalGB, set up as a weak
Pikachu against a level 20 Weedle. The footage starts at the right moment for Pikachu's HP
to hit zero just as she shrieks.

## When the mouth opens

A maths creature can have a second drawing for a part. Charizard's head has two: mouth shut
and mouth open. Lip-sync is choosing one of them for every frame.

Two things decide it. First, the words: whisper, running locally, gives every word a start
and an end. Second, the loudness, measured at each of the video's 30 frames a second. The
mouth opens when the voice is on inside a word, or anywhere it is loud enough to count as a
shout.

That alone gives a mouth that hangs open through a whole sentence. Real mouths close between
syllables, and those closes show up as dips in the loudness. So any frame a few decibels
quieter than the peaks either side of it shuts the mouth. Then the flapping is cleaned up: no
opening shorter than three frames, no gap shorter than two.

## How the body acts

The mouth is automatic. The acting is not, quite. We watched her a quarter of a second at a
time and wrote down what she does, as a beat sheet: a start, an end, a gesture and how much
of it. This one has 52 lines.

There are fourteen gestures. *Lean* towards the camera. *Recoil*. *Shake* the head, *nod*,
*laugh*, *droop*. *Cover* the face. *Slam* the desk, *flail* the arms, *roar* with the mouth
held open. *Rage* is a judder of the whole body by whole pixels, with the flame roaring.
Each one eases in and out, and when two overlap they add up, so a recoil during a rage is
both at once.

Gestures name his bones by role, neck, head, trunk, flame, so the same sheet could drive
another creature with the bones renamed. The blinks run on their own clock and stay shut
wherever a gesture shuts the eyes.

</section>

One rule shaped every gesture: nothing turns his face. A pixel face turned even slightly
loses its eyes, and with them the whole performance. So a head shake moves the head side to
side by whole pixels instead of rotating it.

The performance is written out as motions eight seconds long. A track in our format holds at
most 255 keys. Over this minute, the track that stretches his body
needs 1,292. Each motion ends back at rest on the
exact tick the next one starts, so they play as one.

## Flames on the shouts

The loudest moments are found from the sound, the same way as the mouth: every peak above
−30 dB, at least 1.2 seconds after the last one. There are twelve in this clip. The loudest is
−14.8 dB, seven and a half seconds in, screaming into her hands.

Each peak does two things. The tail flame swells by how loud it was and fades over about a
second. And it becomes one line in the short's script: a lick of fire on the cam, taking
turns round the four corners, bigger for a louder shout.

## What doesn't work yet

His open-mouth drawing is angry. Half of what she does in this clip is laugh, and when she
laughs he looks furious.

His arms are stubs. She covers her face with her hands several times, and his hands cannot
reach his face, so in this tight framing the covering is done by the head and the eyes
instead.

The fire licks are smooth shapes on a pixel creature, and they look it.

And in the lunge he grows so big his headset runs off the top of the frame.

## Any creature with a second mouth

Nothing here is Charizard-specific. Any creature with a second drawing for its mouth can be
handed a recording and a beat sheet and perform it. The way of working, a quarter of a second
at a time against whisper's word timings, came from an earlier remake in which Psyduck played
Joe Biden in an interview clip. This time the mouth worked itself out.

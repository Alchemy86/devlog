AgentGB

Teaching a small local network to play Pokémon Blue from the screen alone — a picture in, a button out, on any emulator, at zero inference cost. Pokémon Blue has already been beaten by AI. It was beaten expensively, by a frontier model reading a text description of the game through a Lua bridge into one specific emulator. This project is about the part nobody has done.

Two jobs, and only one of them is a learning problem

Reinforcement learning on this problem asks one network to learn two different jobs at once — where to go and what am I looking at — both from the same scalar reward. AgentGB splits those apart and gives each one the tool that is already good at it.

  • Where to go is a search problem, and search is solved. A shortest-path algorithm over a walkable graph does it. No network is asked to rediscover pathfinding.
  • What am I looking at is a perception problem, and perception is what networks are good at. A network does that, and only that.

That is where the numbers come from. The student never has to work out where to go — the training label already answers it — so its entire job is to see, which is a far smaller problem than the one an end-to-end agent is handed. It is why the shipped player is a hundred thousand parameters rather than millions.

The price, in the same breath as the benefit

The split works only because the teacher half is allowed what the student never is: the cartridge's memory, and a map walked in advance. An end-to-end agent needs neither. That is the trade this design makes, and it is stated on the project's own front page above every result rather than in a footnote below them.

The split itself is not novel either, and the project says so and credits the prior art at length: the PokéAgent Challenge's winning entry decomposes a route into subgoals with executable success conditions, solves each with a scripted policy and distils the result into a network; the third-place entry paired deterministic pathfinding with a language model for everything else. Two of the top three split the same seam.

What the student actually sees

Fences, buildings and tall grass survive the reduction and stay distinct from each other. People survive as small dark blobs — visible, which is the point of pooling 4×4 rather than 8×8, and only just, which is a real limit of this observation and is written up as one rather than glossed.

There is one measurement that makes the case for pixels better than any argument about representation. An earlier student read the cartridge's memory as a feature vector. Letting the map's own people wander for up to 3,000 frames before an episode starts — rather than the 0–96 frames a state pool frozen from one traversal gives you — took that student from 100 % to 52.7 % on the same policy at the same tile, 150 attempts. A person standing in the way is drawn. It does not need a feature describing them, and the screen-only student pays nothing measurable for the same perturbation: across the nine chain links, at 3,000 attempts in each spread, the wide and narrow counts are identical link for link, with one exception — the off-chain catch in Mt Moon, 2,585 wide against 2,633 narrow.

Where the parameters actually are

The thing a reader usually does not expect is the shape of the bars. 86 % of the model is the single dense layer between the last convolution and the 128-unit trunk — 102,400 weights of 118,806. The three convolutions together are 13 %. The perception machinery everybody pictures when they hear "convolutional network" is the cheap part; the expensive part is the flattening.

The file that ships is a little larger than the inference core, because it is goal-conditioned: on top of the 118,806 it carries a 5×32 pair of conditioning coefficients and a four-way goal head, so the network can derive which part of the chain it is in rather than being told. That is 122,330 weights and one metadata string — counted, not quoted, from the committed file.

The floor: what no learning at all looks like

Before any result means anything there has to be something to beat, and it has to be measured on the same rig rather than assumed.

The floor, measured on this rig and reproducible from a seed:

  • 54.97 % of samples are still in Pallet Town after ten minutes;
  • the northern half of Route 1 is empty in every frame;
  • no agent ever reaches Viridian City — not here, and not in a longer control;
  • 5 % of the tiles visited hold 37 % of the time.

And the reason is a property of the cartridge, not of the policy: Route 1's ledges are a one-way valve. A random walk drifts south through them and cannot climb back — the same mechanism AtlasGB measured on Viridian City's row 27, at scale, with 42 one-way drops on Route 1 alone. Determinism is tested both ways: the same seed in two separate processes produces byte-identical images, a different seed does not.

A rate with no model name attached is not a verified rate

This is the section the rest of the page depends on, and it is not about machine learning at all.

On 21 August 2026, in one evening, this project reported a rate to the captain as belonging to the committed network when it did not — twice. Once because a results page never named which weights produced its headline. Once because a sampling result measured on one student was carried onto a different one. Both reports were plausible, both were wrong, and nothing in the repository could have caught either.

The fix is not "be more careful next time"; it is a check that fails a build the same way it failed the captain.

tools/docs_check.py, its own docstring

A page opts in by carrying a literal marker in its first forty lines. Every Markdown table on an opted-in page that contains a percentage-looking cell is then a rate table, and each one independently must carry one of: a per-row attribution column headed student, policy, network, model, arm or tag; a weights filename in its own rows; or the literal word unattributed. A short, bounded look-back above the table counts, so a caption directly above one still works.

The opt-in is deliberate. Most tables in the repository are about the teacher — a scripted program, never versioned by weights — or about cartridge mechanics, and neither has this ambiguity. Only pages reporting a trained network's own success rate need to say which network.

The best part is the bug in the checker itself. An earlier version carried a sticky "have we seen a model name anywhere in this file yet" flag — which meant a single mention in a page's opening paragraph made every table below it, no matter how far below and no matter what it was about, unfailable. That was caught by deleting a real table's attribution column and watching the check still pass. Each table is now judged on its own.

The general lesson this repository draws from it, and the reason this page carries a model name beside almost every figure: this project names its models, and a rate without a model name attached is not a verified rate.

What the committed student actually does, measured directly

A worked example of the rule above, because it also overturned an assumption.

The obvious explanation for the starter distribution the shipped student produces was that an earlier corpus-balancing fix explains it. That answer was given before anyone checked whether the current weights were trained on the balanced corpus at all — so it was checked, two candidate explanations at a time.

Candidate one, confirmed: the training history records which corpus each link's gradient came from, and the file's SHA-256 is byte-identical to the balanced one. Candidate two, refuted: the entropy at the chain's entry state is not the near-zero collapse an earlier arm showed. Measured directly against models/pixel-student.npz (sha256 b2bb79082c88c486c8d6e146be000627a59af3efaa19a9fc75641e957262c109) at the entry state, the first decision is a genuine two-way split — down 0.590, right 0.408, 0.992 bits of entropy out of a possible 2.585.

And then the full-episode outcome rather than one decision. pixeleval take-a-starter --policy models/pixel-student.npz --split entry --settle-party 8 --attempts 500 --goal 0, default driver, sampled at temperature 1.0, N = 500:

The instrument turned on itself

On 18 August 2026 this project spent a day adding no capability whatsoever. What it did instead was prove that two numbers it had already published were not readable — and every result since is trustworthy because of it.

The training run is the largest term in every ablation, and it had never been controlled for. Five runs of the identical configuration on one link scored:

Run of an identical configuration12345
Cold success rate100.00 %92.33 %92.33 %84.63 %77.00 %

A 23-point band, most of it the seed and the rest the BLAS thread count. Every ablation published before that day lacked the control, which means every one of them could have been measuring the draw rather than the change. A fit does not reproduce: same seed, same recipe, a differently-loaded machine, and you get different weights.

The second finding from the same day is the reason the wide/narrow split exists at all. Cold evaluation over a start-state pool is optimistic, by about a factor of two on a map with people in it. Every state in a pool is frozen from one traversal, so the map's own people barely move between them: same policy, same tile, 150 attempts — 100 % under a 0–96 frame start offset, 52.7 % under 0–3,000. Six distinct people-layouts against twenty-two. Every rate published since carries both spreads, because the gap between them is the honest measure of how brittle a student is.

The chain collapsed to 7 of 300, and it was a label bug

Adding the three links that leave Pallet Town made a nine-link chain collapse from 300 of 300 to 7 of 300. Every one of the 293 lost runs stopped in the same room, in the same posture, on the same text box: Oak's Lab, tile (5,3), "Gramps! What about me?" — the student pressing a direction at a text box for 400 decisions out of 400 without moving a tile.

The cause was one missing rule in the teacher, not anything about the student. Every teacher in the repository answers an open text box with B — except the navigation teacher, which gated that rule on a mode flag, and that flag reads "overworld" while a box is on the screen. For five links nothing noticed, because no navigation link had ever been in a room with a conversation in it. The link that leaves the lab is the first, and it put thousands of frames of an open text box into the corpus labelled with a direction. A direction press re-triggers the box. The student learned that faithfully and did it four hundred times.

It was a label bug, not a perception bug. The picture was fine and the student did exactly what its supervisor said. The fix is one shared helper; the blast radius was measured rather than assumed — 12.84 % label drift on the link that leaves the lab and 1.43–3.63 % on the four other navigation links — and both the before and the after are published, because the after is not legible without the before.

Both figures in that headline belong to an earlier, uncommitted arm of the student which always took Charmander, and the project's own front page says so above the table. They are quoted here as a before-and-after on one training arm, which is what they are, and not as a score for the file that ships.

The one failure in twelve thousand, run down rather than counted

Four cells of one certification table are 3,000 attempts each — two links, two spreads — and exactly one attempt of the twelve thousand fails. That is the only evidence the pair has produced about how it can still go wrong, so it was chased instead of rounded away.

It reproduces exactly. A certification's start states are a pure function of the link, the held-out split, the seed and the spread, so no rollout has to be kept for an attempt to be run again: it is attempt #705 of 3,000, held-out start (31,26), start offset 1,772 frames. Re-run, it fails the same way. The fifteen other attempts from the same tile all reach the goal in 30 to 67 decisions.

The anatomy of it is unusually complete, and every alternative explanation was closed by measurement rather than by argument:

  • Not a starved wanderer waiting it out.   • Not the bag check. The run spends 301 of its 600 decisions with a menu on screen, but the menus are not what pins the person: the arm that presses only b runs the overworld continuously for 533 decisions and the person does not move either.

The condition on these figures, carried from the source. Every rate for this link predates a corpus re-collection on 23 Aug 2026: the round-0 corpus it was trained from carried 105 leaked frames, a fresh corpus collected under the fix audits clean, and no student has been retrained or re-certified on it. The results page's own attribution column reads unattributed for these rows — the certification tooling does not yet write which weights it ran into the certification file — and that is recorded as an honest gap rather than filled in from a directory convention. This account is a measurement of one specific reproducible attempt, flagged, not re-measured.

Reading the screen without reading the letters

At 40×36, individual letterforms do not survive. So the natural question — can this observation tell one message from another at all? — got measured, and the first two answers were both wrong, in the same way.

Attempt one tested two message pairs at N = 5 and got balanced accuracy 0.50: chance. Attempt two sharpened the question and got 0.33: still chance. Both verdicts were undermined by the same thing — a sample of three to five examples per class, which lets a probe predict one class always and look like chance.

Attempt three used real N: dozens of genuine frames per message, taken from the typing animation itself, over six distinct in-game messages. Nearest-centroid across all six identities, with a temporal split — train on each message's first 65 % of typing, test on the last 35 % it never saw — gives 97.2 % accuracy against 20.0 % chance, N = 36 held out, permutation p < 0.005. The confusion matrix is essentially the identity matrix.

What survives is shape, not letters — line length, word-wrap position, letter density per region. And a background confound was checked for, found, and shown not to matter: one of the six messages had to be captured from a separate session, and its background differs from the other five by 14 of 1,440 cells, all inside one small icon. Removing that message entirely, or masking those 14 cells, moves the headline by about one point.

Unattributed on purpose: these are fresh linear probes and nearest-centroid classifiers fit on frozen observations, not a trained student network, so there is no weights file to name.

The structural version of the same finding is the one that mattered in practice. Compare the bag opened with an empty item pocket against the same bag holding Oak's parcel: 57 cells of the 40×36 observation separate the two states perfectly, and none of them require reading a letter. A goal marker does not have to be legible text; a reliable change in layout is enough.

Where these numbers come from

The parameter counts on this page were counted, not quoted. Loading models/pixel-student.npz with NumPy and summing .size over the arrays gives: three convolutions 15,504 · the dense layer 102,528 (102,400 weights plus 128 biases) · the six-way head 774 — 118,806 for the inference core, which is 86.3 % dense layer and 13.1 % convolution. The committed goal-conditioned file adds 320 conditioning coefficients and a 3,204-parameter goal head for 122,330 weights plus one metadata string, and its own metadata records in_shape [4, 36, 40], n_actions 6, observation "screen". Its sha256 is b2bb79082c88c486c8d6e146be000627a59af3efaa19a9fc75641e957262c109.

Every rate here carries the condition it was measured under, because this project has been burned by a rate that did not. The starter distribution names its policy file, its sha256, its command, its sample size (N=500) and its temperature (1.0). The 23-point band and the 100 %/52.7 % spread are the project's own published controls. The chain figures (7 of 300 and 300 of 300) belong to an earlier uncommitted arm and are labelled as such. The one-failure account carries its corpus caveat and its unattributed status. The swarm baseline involves no weights at all.

All four images are committed artefacts of real runs in the AgentGB repository, regenerated by named tools, and copied here byte-for-byte apart from PNG re-compression (verified pixel-identical). Game art is Nintendo and Game Freak's and appears here as documentation of a measurement; the project ships no cartridge, no save and no game data, and every frame is rendered from a cartridge the user supplies.
